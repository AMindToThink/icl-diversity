Why measure diversity?

The possibility for diverse outputs is necessary, but not sufficient, for creativity. Researchers routinely need to compare the diversity of outputs across:

  • elicitation methods during propensity and capability evaluations, to get a complete picture of what the model is capable of and what it might choose to do
  • post-training stages of the same base model (mode collapse)
  • decoding strategies (what does temperature trade off against?)
  • human–AI hybrid pipelines (how can AI be used to amplify rather than weaken human creativity?)

Existing metrics rely on embedding distances or surface-level n-gram statistics, both of which lack a human-like ability to recognize arbitrary patterns.

The idea: in-context learning as a lens

We exploit the in-context learning capability described by Brown et al. (2020), applied here as a measurement lens rather than as a few-shot-prompting setup. As \(\theta\) sees more responses, its in-context learning picks up on shared modes, stylistic regularities, and topic patterns.

If responses from a policy \(\pi\) are diverse, seeing one should not help \(\theta\) predict the next, whereas if they are repetitive or constrained to a few modes, conditioning on earlier responses should sharply reduce \(\theta\)’s surprise at later ones.

Diversity is therefore a property of (responses, prompt, scoring model). The metric measures diversity as \(\theta\) perceives it.

Takeaways
  • A base model’s in-context learning detects similarities between arbitrary numbers of responses in a single forward pass per permutation.
  • Measuring diversity with ICL requires no special-purpose training, datasets, or predetermined dimensions of diversity.
  • The same pipeline scores AI samples and human-written sets, and tracks post-training diversity loss on OLMo-2-7B.

Code & data
Method: the Decan score \(D_{Ca_n} = C \times a_n\)

Decan is read off the per-token log-probabilities of a base model \(\theta\), in one forward pass per permutation.

Progressive surprise \(a_k = -\log_2 \theta(r_k \mid r_{<k}, p)\) (bits/byte)
How surprised \(\theta\) is by response \(k\) given the previous \(k{-}1\). It is averaged over random response orderings (permutations). The curve of \(a_k\) values characterizes diversity, and scalar summaries attempt to capture its shape.
Residual surprise \(a_n = H(r_n \mid p) - I(r_n; r_{<n} \mid p)\) (bits/byte)
The curve’s last point: the response’s own surprise minus what the others reveal about it. High \(a_n\) needs responses to remain surprising even after seeing the rest.
Coherence \(C = 1 / \mathrm{PPL}_\theta(\pi, p)\) (unitless)
Reciprocal perplexity suppresses the diversity score of incoherent sets because noise should not make the metric consider the set to be more diverse.
Decan \(D_{Ca_n} = C \times a_n\) (bits/byte)
Bits of surprise per byte left after \(\theta\) learns what it can from the other responses, weighted by coherence.
Human Writing Diversity Evaluation

We evaluate on Tevet and Berant’s human diversity benchmark. The response sets are written by Mechanical Turk workers, with the high/low diversity label fixed by each dataset’s construction protocol.

Across the binary tasks Decan clearly tracks human diversity judgements, between 3.7% and 21.0% behind SentBERT in OCA, reaching close to a sentence-embedding baseline.

Spearman \(\rho\) and OCA against human diversity labels. \(C \times a_n\) and \(a_n\) are ours (Qwen2.5-3B, 50 permutations, per-byte); baselines are from Tevet and Berant. Best per task in bold.

AI writing evaluation: Post-training mode collapse

On the OLMo-2-7B post-training pipeline, Decan decreases monotonically across the base, SFT, DPO, and RLVR stages, detecting the type of diversity loss that creative-writing applications care about.

base 0.481 → SFT 0.329 → DPO 0.286 ≈ RLVR 0.281

All three pre-registered contrasts are significant after Bonferroni correction.

AI writing evaluation: Agreement with existing metrics

Per-prompt Decan also agrees with the lexical (EAD) and semantic (SentBERT) baselines on the same length-matched subset, confirming the metrics see the same diversity-loss signal.
