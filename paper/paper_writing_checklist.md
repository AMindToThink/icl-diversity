# ML Paper Writing Checklist

Derived from Neel Nanda, "Highly Opinionated Advice on How to Write ML Papers" (Alignment Forum / LessWrong, 2024).

Source: https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/eJGptPbbFPZGLpjsp/highly-opinionated-advice-on-how-to-write-ml-papers

Apply to `in_context_diversity_metric.tex`. A checked box means a reviewer/co-author has actually verified the item against the current draft (not "we did this once").

---

## Conventions

Ticked items point to the prose they refer to using one of two forms:

- **Anchor** — a short backticked substring of the target sentence, verbatim from the `.tex` source. Locate it with VS Code's Find-in-File, or `rg -nF '<anchor>' paper/in_context_diversity_metric.tex`. Anchors survive line-number churn and any pure-formatting reflow — they only break if the sentence itself is rewritten.
- **Label** — a backticked LaTeX label like `` `sec:scalar` `` or `` `eq:D-ainf` `` for section-, figure-, and equation-level items. Locate it with `rg '\\label\{sec:scalar\}' paper/`.

Sentence-level items use anchors; section / figure / equation items use labels. The `.tex` source is kept in one-sentence-per-line form, so each anchor resolves to a single line.

When ticking a new item, add the relevant anchor or label at the end of the bullet after an em-dash:
`- [x] Bullet text. — \`anchor phrase\`` or `- [x] Bullet text. — \`sec:some-label\``.

## 1. Core narrative & claims

- [x] Paper compresses to **1–3 specific, concrete novel claims** that share a single coherent theme. *(Three-claim compression settled on 2026-04-25: primary=$C \times a_n$ is a principled, well-behaved per-byte diversity metric (methodological); secondary=competitive with embedding-based metrics on Tevet's diversity-eval benchmark (empirical credibility); tertiary=detects post-training diversity collapse on OLMo-2-7B (practical demonstration). Cross-model scaling (D) remains in the paper as supporting evidence, not a headline. Theme: base-model in-context-learning as a principled diversity signal.)* — `progressive conditional surprise is a principled complement`
- [x] Each claim is of the form "X is best on Y", "behavior A is explained by mechanism B", or similar — no fuzzy "we explore" claims. *(Contributions bullets use "We define", "We introduce", "We validate" — concrete verbs; abstract closer says "is a principled complement", not "we explore". Every validation sentence states a specific numeric or directional outcome.)* — `\paragraph{Contributions.}`
- [x] Each claim is stated at the **right confidence level** (existence proof / systematic / hedged / narrow / guarantee) and the language used in the paper matches that level. *(Primary="principled complement" — confident, backed by 4 fronts; secondary="competitive with", not "best" — hedged to match the Tevet trail behind SentBERT; tertiary="detects diversity loss" — existence-proof language on one model family.)* — `competitive with embedding-based metrics`
- [x] Claims are **explicitly distinguished** from prior work — it is obvious to a reader what is novel. *(§Motivation contrasts embedding (du2019boosting) and $n$-gram baselines, then explicitly contrasts the pairwise-MI line (li2016diversity, zhang2018aim) as "a training or decoding objective rather than an evaluation-time diagnostic". §Related Concepts positions NoveltyBench and Tevet framework as parallel lines with specific methodological contrasts.)* — `A complementary line of work applies information theory directly`; `sec:related`
- [x] Narrative has been **red-teamed**: we actively searched for evidence that contradicts each claim before writing. *(Positive red-teaming evidence: §Limitations now documents 7+ caveats including the "Metric selection saw the full Tevet benchmark" disclosure (added 2026-04-25; notes OLMo is independent, mode-count is informally-selecting). §Tevet documents the "last-position uptick" anomaly with a working hypothesis. §8.1 documents the McDiv dataset-construction confound. A stage-level length audit on OLMo (`scripts/audit_response_lengths.py`) showed raw length is non-monotone across stages, and the subsequent correctness check identified that $C$ and $a_n$ are both within-response-ICL-sensitive, so per-byte scoring does not automatically insulate $D$ from length. The length-matched re-run scoped in `investigations/length_matched_rlhf/README.md` was executed 2026-04-25 (see `investigations/length_matched_rlhf/VERDICT.md`): per-prompt truncation to a common UTF-8 byte length, re-scored with the same Qwen2.5-3B base; the monotone $D$ drop survives on both prompt sets (`Base→Instruct` raw $0.186$ → length-matched $0.199$ on AlpacaEval, $n=150/200$ kept; raw $0.202$ → length-matched $0.179$ on NB-curated, $n=39/100$ kept; all three H1 contrasts remain Bonferroni-significant $p<0.001$). 111/300 prompts were dropped because $L_{\mathrm{prompt}} < 50$ bytes (mostly base-on-AlpacaEval and SFT-on-NB-curated stop-token-early cases) — the length-matched effect therefore conditions on prompts where length-matching is well-defined. The §Limitations paragraph "Length sensitivity of $D$" records this.)* — `sec:limitations`
- [x] Claims survive the "skeptical engaged reader" test — every section earns its place by supporting a claim. *(Method sections (§Setup, §Progressive Conditioning, §Coherence, §Reporting the Metric) define the primary claim's object. §Experiments subsections each support a specific front: §Scenario Validation + §Mode Count Scaling (primary, "works in both regimes"), §Cross-Mode Learning (supporting, D), §External Validation/Tevet (secondary), §RLHF-Diversity (tertiary). §Practical Considerations is reproducibility support for the primary. §Limitations, §Future Work, §Related Concepts are Nanda-checklist required.)* — `sec:experiments`

## 2. Evidence & experimental rigor

- [x] For each claim, we can name the **single experiment** that most cleanly supports it. *(Primary ($C \times a_n$ principled/well-behaved): §Scenario Validation. Secondary (Tevet external validation): §External Validation / McDiv prompt_gen AUC. Tertiary (OLMo post-training collapse): §RLHF-Diversity four-stage contrasts. All three are linked from the abstract and Contributions list.)* — `\paragraph{Contributions.}`
- [ ] Critical experiments have been **re-implemented through an alternate pathway** (different code path, different model, different metric, etc.) — at least 75% verification. *(Explicitly scoped out 2026-04-25: reimplementing core experiments goes beyond 20/80 value for this paper.)*
- [ ] Statistical thresholds are appropriate for exploratory ML work (target **p < 0.001**, not p < 0.05); sample sizes, std devs, and noise levels are reported. *(RLHF contrasts report $d_z$ + Bonferroni $p$ for all three pre-registered contrasts (H1a/H1b/H1c) on both prompt sets, inline via macros (`\olmoAlpacaHoneADz` etc., added 2026-04-25); all $p_\mathrm{Bonf} = 0.000$ exceeds the $p<0.001$ target. Cross-mode pairwise matrix reports per-cell $\pm$std. Mode-count Pearson $r$ reports $N=1000$ draws per mode count. **Remaining gaps (deferred):** Tevet AUC values lack bootstrap CIs; mode-count $r$ lacks a CI. Not critical — consistent pattern across 12 task $\times$ dataset combos and AUC margins to SentBERT are $\sim$5–5.5 SE on McDiv ($N\approx$2K), so point estimates carry the load. Add if a reviewer asks.)* — `pre-registered paired tests`; `Cell standard deviations are the mean within-cell`
- [x] Pre-hoc vs. post-hoc analyses are **clearly labeled** — no quietly post-hoc explanations dressed as predictions. *(H1a/H1b/H1c explicitly labeled "pre-registered paired tests" in table caption and discussion. H1$'$ (DPO vs RLVR) labeled "exploratory two-sided contrast". Last-position uptick flagged as "working hypothesis". Metric-selection bias disclosed in §Limitations ("Metric selection saw the full Tevet benchmark"). Mode-count experiments disclosed as informally influencing the metric choice (not strictly independent validation). BPE-boundary as a technical note.)* — `pre-registered paired tests`; `Metric selection saw the full Tevet benchmark`; `our working hypothesis is that 4 responses genuinely form`
- [x] **Cherry-picking guard:** any qualitative example is paired with **randomly selected** examples for context. *(Scenario-validation response sets are disclosed as deliberately constructed with known structure, not a subset. McDiv per-token figures (`fig:confound-high`, `fig:confound-low`) now have an explicit selection-method sentence in §mcdiv-examples: samples are ranked by per-byte $a_1$ within each label group and drawn at the $\lfloor N/4 \rfloor$ position — representative of the expected confound direction, not extreme outliers; pinned IDs fixed in `scripts/dataset_confound_analysis.py` for reproducibility (added 2026-04-25). Distributional figure `fig:confound-a1-dist` accompanies the per-token examples.)* — `Samples are drawn by ranking each label group`
- [x] **Ablations** isolate each component (one variable at a time). *(Tables 1 and 2 now include three "(ours)" rows — $C \times a_n$, $a_n$ alone, $C$ alone — across ConTest / McDiv_nuggets / McDiv × {prompt_gen, resp_gen, story_gen} and decTest (added 2026-04-25 via `scripts/analyze_c_ainf.py` + `scripts/build_paper_macros.py`, with the $C$ row computed from raw float `coherence_C` values, not rounded cells). On the nine binary (crowdsourced) tasks, $C \times a_n$ beats $a_n$ alone on 18/18 entries and $C$ alone on 17/18 (one McDiv_nuggets story_gen OCA cell where $C$ narrowly wins, honestly disclosed in the "Alternative scalars" paragraph). The `\tevetCVsCxAnOCAMinPct` / `\tevetCVsCxAnOCAMaxPct` macros carry the ablation gap range.)* — `Both factors of $D = C \times a_n$ contribute`
- [x] Baselines are **strong and well-optimized** — we put as much effort into competitor methods as into ours. *(Baselines are Tevet's own pre-computed metrics (SentBERT, BERTsts, BERTScore, distinct-$n$, cos-sim), loaded from `diversity-eval/data/with_metrics/` — the established competitors in that benchmark, not hand-rolled. Provenance documented in a `% Baseline source:` comment in both generated tables. On the RLHF experiment, EAD and SentBERT-similarity-to-diversity act as cross-metric checks on the same generations.)* — `% Baseline source: diversity-eval/data/with_metrics/`
- [x] Multiple **qualitatively different lines of evidence** point to the same conclusion (not just one type of evidence multiplied). *(Five distinct evidence types support the primary claim: (1) synthetic scenario validation (constructed ground truth), (2) synthetic mode-count scaling ($C \times a_n$ Pearson $r$ vs $m$, two different base models), (3) cross-mode pairwise matrix (information-theoretic structure of $a_k$), (4) external crowdsourced validation on Tevet binary tasks (ROC AUC / $\rho$ / OCA), (5) OLMo-2-7B post-training pipeline (paired pre-registered Wilcoxon). Plus cross-model Llama scaling for supporting evidence on $D$.)* — `sec:experiments`
- [x] We have asked ourselves: *"How surprised would I be if this were complete bullshit due to a bug, error, noise, or misunderstanding?"* — and the answer is "very." *(User-judged: very surprised. Reasoning: 90% AUC on McDiv_nuggets prompt_gen competitive with SentBERT would be hard to fake from a bug — the consistent 12-cell pattern across Tevet binary tasks + monotone $D$ drop across four OLMo stages + monotone mode-count scaling on two different base models represent five independent failure modes that would all have to conspire. Known non-bullshit anomalies (last-position uptick, McDiv-nuggets story_gen $C$-alone anomaly) are tied to dataset construction, not metric bugs. Length-sensitivity concern for OLMo addressed via the length-matched re-run (see `investigations/length_matched_rlhf/VERDICT.md`).)*

## 3. Reproducibility

- [x] Sufficient detail is given that an outside researcher can replicate the work. *(Paper specifies base models by HF ID, permutation counts, $n$, prompt format; §Practical Considerations gives the full conditioning format.)* — `sec:practical`
- [x] Hyperparameters, implementation details, and "fiddly bits" are specified (in main text or clearly referenced appendix). *(Decoding parameters and per-experiment $n$ / $n_\sigma$ in figure captions; base-model versions in §Experimental Setup.)* — `sec:exp-setup`
- [x] Code is shared with a **helpful README** (setup, commands, expected outputs). *(Repo root `README.md` has concept table, quickstart, and per-script commands; project CLAUDE.md enumerates reproduction commands.)*
- [x] Datasets and model weights are released (e.g., via Hugging Face) where applicable. *(Base models are all public; the OLMo-2-7B four-stage generations are released at `huggingface.co/datasets/AMindToThink/olmo-2-1124-7b-four-stage-samples-rlhf-diversity` and linked in the "Released artifacts" paragraph of rlhf_experiment.tex.)* — `AMindToThink/olmo-2-1124-7b-four-stage-samples-rlhf-diversity`
- [x] At least one **demonstration notebook / script** lets a reader reproduce a key result. *(`scripts/run_scenarios.py`, `scripts/plot_ak_curves.py`, `scripts/analyze_c_ainf.py`, `scripts/interactive_scatter.py` — each tied to a specific paper result.)*
- [x] Every numeric claim in prose resolves to a script-generated source (table, macro, JSON) — no hand-typed numbers. *(Project-specific: `paper_macros.tex`, `bibliography-from-ids` discipline.)* *(Enforced by `tests/test_paper_macros.py`; 6/6 pass as of 2026-04-24.)*

## 4. Abstract

- [x] **Sentence 1:** an uncontroversially true statement that pins down which subfield of ML this paper lives in. — `Measuring the diversity of language-model outputs`
- [x] **Sentence 2:** signals the gap or problem this paper addresses. — `Existing diversity metrics rely on embedding-space`
- [x] **Sentences 3–4:** state the main contribution with the minimal definitions a reader needs. — `We develop an alternative grounded in information theory`; `This yields a \emph{progressive conditional surprise curve}`
- [x] **Following 1–2 sentences each:** any additional key claim or experimental result. *(validation split into 4 sentences, one per result.)* — `We validate the metric on four fronts`
- [x] At least one **concrete metric or numeric result** is in the abstract to make the result feel real. *(\tevetMcDivPromptGenAUC on McDiv prompt\_gen.)* — `ROC AUC up to \tevetMcDivPromptGenAUC`
- [x] **Final 1–2 sentences:** why this matters / broader context, with the standard of evidence honestly stated. *(closer added; chose "show" rather than "preliminary" — four-front validation does not warrant preliminary-tier hedging.)* — `Together, these results show`
- [x] One idea per sentence — no run-on or overstuffed sentences. *(S6 on $D = C \times a_\infty$ was split into three: definition, "does not reward noise", "works in both regimes".)* — `Their product $D_{a_\infty} = C \times a_\infty$`

## 5. Introduction

The paper's §Motivation plays this role (no separate \section{Introduction}).

- [x] **Paragraph 1 (Context):** defines the topic, motivating question, and why it matters; cites liberally to establish this is a real field. *(Topic and applications stated in L55–56; existing-approach citations in L57–58.)* — `Consider evaluating the diversity of outputs`
- [x] **Paragraph 2 (Background):** explains established techniques the work builds on; situates the problem; identifies what's inadequate in existing approaches. *(Combined with P1 in the opening paragraph — embedding/$n$-gram metrics cited, then their limitations spelled out at the end of the same paragraph.)* — `These existing approaches have known limitations`
- [x] **Paragraph 3 (Contribution):** main claim stated with nuance, detail, and explicit novel-vs-prior-work delineation. *(Contribution stated in L61; pairwise-MI delineation handled earlier via contrast with li2016diversity / zhang2018aim.)* — `We propose an alternative grounded in information theory`
- [ ] **Paragraph 3.5 (Evidence):** summarizes the strongest empirical support for the claim. *(Partially covered by the new contributions list, which names the four validation fronts; a dedicated evidence paragraph would make the intro self-contained for readers who skip the abstract.)*
- [ ] (Repeat 3 + 3.5 for each secondary claim.) *(Not applicable — the paper has a single primary claim with several supporting results, not multiple peer claims.)*
- [ ] **Paragraph 4 (Impact):** articulates takeaways and who should change behavior because of this paper. *(Missing from intro. Abstract closer and §sec:exp-discussion carry the impact, but a short intro-level version would help readers who stop after the intro.)*
- [x] Closes with a **bulleted contributions list** giving each claim + brief evidence pointer. *(Added before the pipeline figure; three bullets mirroring the abstract's three substantive claims, each pointing at the section that formalizes it.)* — `\paragraph{Contributions.}`

## 6. Methods & results

- [x] A **background section** defines all key terms and techniques — including ones we think are "obvious" (e.g., don't assume readers know what a sparse autoencoder / SAE / specific metric is). *(§Setup and Notation defines $p$, $\pi$, $r_i$, $\theta$, $|r|_\mathrm{tok}$, $\|r\|$, total cross-entropy, conditional cross-entropy, and per-byte cross-entropy rate. ICL is glossed inline in §Motivation's "Important caveat" paragraph. ROC AUC is glossed inline at first use ("the natural metric for binary classification"). OCA was added (2026-04-25) at first use in §Tevet — "optimal classification accuracy: the best accuracy achievable by a one-dimensional threshold separating low- and high-diversity sets" — closing the only load-bearing terminology gap. SAE is mentioned once as an example audience in "Who should care" and is not load-bearing.)* — `optimal classification accuracy`
- [x] **Methods section** explains each approach and why it is relevant to the claim it serves. *(§Why Not Pairwise MI? motivates progressive conditioning by showing pairwise MI cannot detect blends. §Coherence Problem motivates the $C$ term by distinguishing diverse-coherent from random-incoherent. §Does the Curve Already Handle Incoherence? motivates the separation of $C$ from $a_n$. §Reporting the Metric explains why curves vs. triple vs. scalar — each compression discards information for a specific reporting use case.)* — `sec:coherence-problem`
- [x] **Results section** specifies experiments, technical choices, and outcomes; each result is tied to a specific claim. *(§Experimental Setup names base models (Qwen2.5-3B + GPT-2 + Llama 3 family), permutation counts (100 / 50), and points to §Cost for the format. Each experiment subsection (§Scenario Validation, §Mode Count Scaling, §Cross-Mode Learning, §External Validation, §RLHF-Diversity, §Practical Findings) opens with its setup and closes with a paragraph stating what was found.)* — `sec:exp-setup`
- [x] If multiple claim types exist, each evidence style has its own section that explicitly links back to its claim. *(Primary claim (D principled/well-behaved): §Scenario Validation + §Mode Count Scaling + §Cross-Mode Learning. Secondary (Tevet competitive): §External Validation. Tertiary (OLMo collapse): §RLHF-Diversity (`\input{rlhf_experiment}`). Each is independently labelled and cross-referenced from the Contributions list and §Discussion.)* — `sec:tevet`
- [x] Technical choices are **justified** — "we used X because Y" — not just stated. *(Examples: "We deliberately avoid using $\pi$ to judge its own outputs"; "We use a base (non-instruction-tuned) model as $\theta$ for two reasons. First..."; "We chose the geometric mean over the arithmetic mean... if 9 out of 10 responses are incoherent... $C_\mathrm{AM}$ remains nonzero while $C$ is driven toward zero"; "Per-byte normalization and averaging over random permutations remove these effects: each position averages over all responses".)* — `chose the geometric mean`
- [x] No critical methodological choice is buried in a passing mention. *(Single-pass vs. multi-pass: §sec:cost has a dedicated paragraph with FLOPs scaling. Boundary handling: §Practical Findings has its own paragraph plus regression test reference. Permutation count: §Experimental Setup + §Practical Findings (with empirical evidence). Base vs. instruction-tuned $\theta$: §Practical Considerations has a dedicated paragraph. Per-byte normalization: defined in §Setup, justified at every use site. Fixed prompt format: explicitly flagged in §Limitations and §Future Work as a deliberate scope choice.)* — `sec:practical`
- [x] Dense technical detail moved to appendices is **clearly cross-referenced** from the main text. *(All six appendices are referenced: app:k0-derivation (4×, including the sigmoid prediction in §Progressive Conditional Surprise and the alternative-scalars discussion); app:excess-entropy (6×, including scenario table caption, mode-count discussion, Tevet alternative-scalars, and §Related Concepts); app:c-normalization (1×, in §Scalar Summary); app:aggregation (1×, in §What to Report); app:confound (3×, in §Tevet caveats and Tevet results); app:qwen3-comparison (3×, in §Tevet discussion and §Limitations).)* — `app:k0-derivation`

## 7. Figures & tables

- [x] For each figure, we have answered: *"What exactly is the information I would like the reader to take away?"* — and the figure makes that takeaway visually obvious. *(Captions consistently open with the figure's point: "Cross-mode information transfer scales with model size"; "The $a_k$ curves fan out with increasing $m$"; etc.)* — `fig:scaling-crossmode`
- [ ] Visual annotation (arrows, highlights, dark vs. low-opacity lines) directs attention to the key pattern. *(Color-coded decomposition in fig:excess-entropy uses red/blue to separate $I_k$ from $e_k$; pipeline uses colored boxes to distinguish tracks. ## 8. Related work

The paper's §Related Concepts plays this role — label `sec:related`.

- [x] Section explains **why this work differs from or builds on** the most similar prior efforts. — `These approaches are complementary to ours`
- [x] If the work is not strongly novel, parallel work is **acknowledged** and the incremental value is articulated. *(NoveltyBench and Tevet framework explicitly positioned as parallel lines.)* — `NoveltyBench \citep{zhang2025noveltybench}`
- [x] Criticism of prior work is **professional**: explains the methodological flaw and why it matters; does not attack authors. — `unclear whether correlation with their labels validates a metric or merely measures agreement between two model-based scores`
- [x] First-instance citations are credited (cite the originator, not just downstream popularizers). *(Project-specific: this is in our memory as a high-priority rule.)* — `\citet{du2019boosting} introduced the embedding-based approach`
- [x] Placement: upfront only if motivating the paper; otherwise penultimate. *(Penultimate, immediately before the bibliography.)* — `sec:related`
- [x] No performative citation padding — every citation serves reader context. *(Each cite is paired with a specific methodological contrast or acknowledgement.)*

## 9. Discussion, limitations, conclusion

- [x] A dedicated **Limitations** section exists and is honest — it documents constraints we know about, not just generic disclaimers. *(Added as §Limitations with six paragraphs: the new "D = C × a_n is pragmatic, not derived" point plus cross-references to the scattered caveats in §Motivation, §Cross-Mode Learning, §Discussion, §External Validation, and Appendix K0-derivation.)* — `sec:limitations`
- [x] Each limitation is paired with what it means for **how strongly the reader should update**. *(Caveat paragraph explicitly explains "the metric measures diversity relative to the base model's perceptual capabilities, which is a weaker claim than measuring the true diversity of π"; σ_ℓ discussion explains "Its role is diagnostic, not as a standalone diversity score".)* — `which is a weaker claim than measuring the true diversity`
- [x] No overclaiming: language like "compelling", "suggestive", "tentative" is used in proportion to evidence strength. *(Abstract says "competitive with", not "outperforms"; §Discussion uses "partially supported", "have not explored this space systematically", "we expect". The phrase "competitive with embedding-based metrics" is deliberately used in all four places the comparison is drawn — abstract, contributions list, §External Validation, and §Discussion — for consistent framing.)* — `Sigmoid prediction partially supported`
- [x] **Future work** identifies genuinely exciting directions, not filler. *(Four concrete paragraphs: dimension-specific prompts, prompt optimization, ensembling, explicit framing. Each is a specific, implementable direction.)* — `\section{Future Work}`
- [x] Conclusion is omitted if it would only repeat the introduction (often optional). *(No conclusion section — abstract closer and contributions list carry the takeaway.)*

## 10. Appendices

- [ ] Appendices hold full hyperparameters, extended ablations, supplementary analyses, and any tacit knowledge / failure modes / replication tricks.
- [ ] A glossary of key terms is included if space pressures forced abbreviated main-text definitions.
- [ ] Main text references each appendix where appropriate ("see Appendix C").
- [ ] We accept that appendices are "low stakes" — they exist for the rare careful reader, not for general polish.

## 11. Prose & language

- [ ] **Plain language preferred**; jargon used only where it adds precision, never for sounding smart.
- [ ] Verbose / overly complex sentences have been cut on at least one editing pass.
- [ ] **Illusion of transparency** has been countered: we re-read assuming the reader has none of our context.
- [ ] Confidence language (compelling / suggestive / preliminary / tentative) matches evidence strength everywhere it appears.
- [ ] We are trying to **inform, not persuade** — no rhetorical inflation of significance.

## 12. Process & quality control

- [ ] We started with a **compression pass**: verbal description → "what was most interesting?" → 1–3 claims with crucial experiments per claim, **before** writing prose.
- [ ] Bullet-point narrative was reviewed by a second person before being expanded.
- [ ] Introduction outline received **outside feedback** before full prose was written.
- [ ] Full paper outline ("convince a skeptical engaged reader") was reviewed before figures were finalized.
- [ ] Time was allocated **comparably** across {abstract, intro, figures, everything else} — not 90% on body and 10% on abstract.
- [ ] At least two iterative editing passes for clarity, narrative tightness, and fluff removal.
- [ ] External reader feedback obtained at least once on the full draft.
- [ ] Final pass: every sentence answers "is this earning its place in the narrative?"
