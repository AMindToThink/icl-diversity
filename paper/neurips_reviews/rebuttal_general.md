Dear Reviewers,

Thank you so much for your thoughts. You all have good points. 

AI is useful, but at the same time researchers wish to engage with the ideas of real people. Out of respect for the reviewers, any AI contributions to these rebuttals are clearly marked between **[AI]** and **[/AI]** markers, and human authors have ensured the correctness of the AI contributions. Content outside of such tags is human-written.

Many of you were concerned by the fact that Decan underperforms the SentBERT baseline.  While it is true that we would have loved our new approach to outperform existing popular approaches in the first paper, our early experiments did not reach that level. They do, however, demonstrate that in-context learning is a promising new foundation for measuring diversity. We wish to publish our findings not because we have the final word on the subject of in-context learning diversity metrics, but because we want to open this area of research for others to develop and build upon. This is in line with NeurIPS' Main Track Handbook, which states that "To help the NeurIPS community move faster out of local minima, it is important to encourage risk and recognize that new approaches can't initially yield state-of-the-art competitive results..." The reviewers all rated our originality favorably, assigning it 3/4/3.

After submitting the paper, we did perform an experiment which demonstrates a case in which in-context learning diversity (in this case, the residual surprise a_n) is superior to SentBERT. When sentence share a structure but have different meanings and different words, in-context learning can pick up on the pattern while neither SentBERT nor n-grams can identify the pattern.

**[AI]** Concretely: every sentence in a set instantiates the same six-word part-of-speech pattern with freshly sampled words (e.g., "Pelicans coughed beyond trolls without senators."), while a matched control draws the same word classes but shuffles each sentence's order, making the control more diverse by a known ground truth of 0.12 bits/byte. Over 20 independent draws (Qwen2.5-3B judge, 40 responses per set), a_n separates the conditions with the correct sign and approximately the correct magnitude (gap 0.174 ± 0.053 bits/byte, p = 0.0024), while SentBERT shows no significant difference (p = 0.11) and distinct-n differs only through a capitalization artifact that vanishes on lowercased text (p = 0.88). We note transparently that the scalar D = C × a_n does not separate these two conditions: the scrambled control is genuinely less coherent, so C offsets its higher a_n as designed, and the structural-redundancy signal is read from the a_k curve and its endpoint a_n. **[/AI]**
